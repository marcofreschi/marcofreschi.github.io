Bio

Marco Freschi is a postdoctoral fellow at the Morphing Matter Lab, Department of Mechanical Engineering, UC Berkeley, working under the direction of Professor Lining Yao. His research focuses on engineered bioinspired self-burying seeds: passive actuators that respond to environmental stimuli without electronics.

After graduating, Marco joined Accenture as a consultant before pursuing his PhD at Politecnico di Milano under the supervision of Professor Giovanni Dotelli, where he studied the tribological and mechanical behavior of metal matrix composites. He then moved to UC Berkeley thanks to a postdoctoral fellowship sponsored by Enel Foundation and managed by Professor Alessandra Lanzara.

His flagship project is a self-burying seed carrier for ecological restoration — a biodegradable wooden structure inspired by the Erodium seed mechanism that drills autonomously into soil using only moisture as its power source. Funded by NSF GCR and featured by CNN and CNN Tech for Good.

Marco has served as PhD Students Representative at Politecnico di Milano and currently as Social Co-chair of the Berkeley Postdoctoral Association.

Marco is actively pursuing faculty and research positions for the 2026–2027 academic year.

Berkeley Postdoctoral Association — As Social Co-chair, Marco manages events for the postdoc community and leads the Queer Coffee Hour: a monthly meetup dedicated to promoting community and culture within the postdoc and visiting scholar community.

Research Interests

My research sits at the intersection of materials science, engineering design, and sustainability. I study how materials behave and fail, engineer systems inspired by nature, and evaluate the full environmental and social life cycle of what I build, with the goal of creating solutions that perform rigorously and last responsibly.

Materials Science

Rigorous & Functional

Data-driven investigation of functional materials across a broad application spectrum — from tribological metal matrix composites to biodegradable morphing systems.

Design Engineering

Applied & Bioinspired

Application-driven research anchored to real requirements. Nature has already solved many of the world's most complex challenges — drawing inspiration from these solutions and translating them into scalable, engineered systems.

Sustainability

Economic · Environmental · Social

Sustainability as a design constraint from day one — life-cycle thinking integrated across every project from raw material extraction to end-of-life.
